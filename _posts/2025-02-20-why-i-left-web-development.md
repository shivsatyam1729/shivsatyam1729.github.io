title: "Why I Left Web Development and What I Gained"

---

I used to build web applications. Full stacks, APIs, interfaces. At some point, I stopped. Not suddenly, but deliberately. 
I didn’t lose anything, I gained a lot of intuition and ideas. Some i would love sharing in the future.

### Why I left

I think my approach to programming was always a little different. I did not leave web development because its industrial value was declining, 
but because of how I wanted to learn and grow.

Designing backend systems and sketching pipelines was enjoyable, but only for a while. After a point, it stopped demanding much thought.
Web development was not forcing me to think deeply. And I realized that is something I value a lot.

### The shift

I am not exceptional at mathematics, but I enjoy it. Unlike web development, it forces me to think and keeps me engaged. I have a habit of sitting with problems for a long time. Some of them remain unsolved. I still keep a log of such problems.

Mathematics felt exciting in a way that was different. Concepts like stochastic models and deterministic machine learning drew me in. Over time, I realized I was moving toward thinking more and building less.

### What I gained

The biggest gain was simple. I felt more aligned with what I was doing. I realized that focus is a powerful thing. When something genuinely holds your attention, it becomes much easier to stay with it for long periods of time. That alone changes how you learn.

I ended up learning a lot more mathematics than I expected. My entry points were things like GMMs, PCA, and SVD through research papers, and I did not find them boring at all. It was exactly the kind of work I wanted to engage with.

In comparison, web development often kept me closer to implementation. It was structured and useful, but it rarely pushed me to think at the level I was beginning to enjoy.

### What I did not lose

I did not really lose anything. If anything, web development gave me a strong sense of implementation. I still remember details from past projects, from design decisions to debugging mistakes, and that carries over.

I still value clean engineering and well designed systems. That has not changed. I would not say that time was wasted. I was exposed to ideas early, even if I did not fully understand them then. Over time, those ideas stayed with me and turned into intuition. I am actually very lucky to have been able to learn structurally from HTML to CSS to JS. 

For example, I learned about sockets quite early through tools like socket.io. I did not revisit them formally, but the intuition remained. Now, working with systems like ZeroMQ feels natural rather than unfamiliar.

### Closing

I try to follow what holds my attention.
Right now, that is machine learning and mathematics. But that could change. Sometimes I come across low level systems work through friends, and it interests me in a different way.

If something is worth thinking about, I will move toward it.
I did not really leave web development. I just became more selective about what I choose to spend my time on.
